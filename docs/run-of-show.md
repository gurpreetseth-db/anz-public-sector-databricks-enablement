# Run of Show

## Session design goal

Deliver a 4-hour live storyline that alternates between context, product action, and audience payoff so energy stays high.

## Agenda at a glance

| Segment | Duration | Primary outcome |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Opening mission and stakes | 10 min | Establish relevance and urgency |
| Act 1: Legacy systems to unified access | 30 min | Show governed access to existing operational data |
| Act 2: File and event ingestion | 30 min | Show raw data arriving quickly and safely |
| Act 3: Transformation and data quality | 40 min | Build trust and operational truth |
| Short break | 10 min | Reset |
| Act 4: Predictive modelling | 30 min | Shift from hindsight to foresight |
| Act 5: Ontology and discoverability | 25 min | Show shared language and governed meaning |
| Act 6: Genie and decision support | 35 min | Broaden access to insight |
| Short break | 10 min | Reset |
| Act 7: Operational application | 25 min | Land the story in frontline action |
| Close and Q&A | 15 min | Reinforce platform coherence |

## Detailed facilitation plan

### Opening mission and stakes
**Goal:** Make the audience care before the platform appears.

Key message:
A major weather event is unfolding. Fire, emergency, health, and infrastructure teams each hold part of the picture. Leaders need one trusted operating view.

### Act 1: Legacy systems to unified access
**Goal:** Show that Databricks can start with what agencies already have.

Walkthrough elements:
* existing incident history from an operational database
* access through governance-aware gateway patterns
* immediate analytical visibility without a disruptive migration discussion

Audience takeaway:
We do not have to rebuild everything first to get value.

### Act 2: File and event ingestion
**Goal:** Introduce new data landing into the platform.

Walkthrough elements:
* weather observations landing as files
* citizen or field reports arriving as JSON
* schema change example to prove real-world messiness is manageable

Audience takeaway:
The platform handles both structured and semi-structured inputs in a public-sector-friendly way.

### Act 3: Transformation and data quality
**Goal:** Show how raw signals become trusted operational data.

Walkthrough elements:
* bronze to silver to gold progression
* validation rules for incident type, coordinates, timestamps, and required fields
* masking or restriction of sensitive reporter details
* gold-level response and resource metrics

Audience takeaway:
Data quality and governance are part of delivery, not post-processing.

### Act 4: Predictive modelling
**Goal:** Show how trusted data supports better prioritisation.

Walkthrough elements:
* risk or severity prediction based on weather, location, and history
* clear explanation of the model's intended use: assist prioritisation, not replace judgment
* optional surfacing of model output back into gold tables or application views

Audience takeaway:
AI is useful when anchored to a specific operational decision.

### Act 5: Ontology and discoverability
**Goal:** Create shared meaning across teams.

Walkthrough elements:
* define critical terms such as incident, severity, response time, containment, and resource deployment
* connect business terms to curated assets and tables
* show how semantic clarity reduces cross-agency confusion

Audience takeaway:
Ontology is not abstract overhead; it reduces decision friction.

### Act 6: Genie and decision support
**Goal:** Let non-technical users ask good questions directly.

Walkthrough elements:
* ask practical executive questions in natural language
* trace answers back to governed data
* optionally show a supporting AI/BI dashboard for command-level monitoring

Audience takeaway:
Insight becomes accessible beyond engineering and BI teams.

### Act 7: Operational application
**Goal:** End on action, not analysis.

Walkthrough elements:
* incident command application concept
* prioritised incidents, response metrics, and resource posture in one view
* explain how this is where platform work reaches an operational user

Audience takeaway:
Databricks is not just a data platform; it can power the operational experience.

## Speaker pacing guidance

* keep setup explanations under 3 minutes before each live action
* narrate the problem, then the product move, then the user outcome
* every 20 to 30 minutes, restate why the audience should care
* use breaks to reset the story arc, not just the clock
