# Session Strategy

## Objective

Win hearts and minds with an end-to-end Databricks story that feels operational, relevant, and achievable for Australia and New Zealand public sector teams.

## Audience assumptions

Likely attendee mix:
* agency data and analytics leaders
* platform owners and enterprise architects
* line-of-business sponsors
* technical practitioners evaluating implementation realism

Likely concerns:
* data sovereignty and trust
* fragmented agency systems
* long delivery cycles
* compliance and governance
* cost versus value
* whether AI can be adopted safely in the public sector

## Positioning principle

Do not lead with features. Lead with an operational mission problem, then reveal Databricks as the platform that makes each step possible.

## Recommended story

**Modern emergency management and disaster response**

Narrative frame:
* multiple agencies hold partial truth
* incoming weather and citizen reports change fast
* leaders need trusted data, not just more data
* analysts need self-service answers
* incident commanders need applications, not raw tables

## Why this storyline wins

* It is immediately legible in ANZ without needing domain explanation.
* It naturally supports batch plus incremental data movement.
* It creates an authentic need for data quality and governance.
* It gives modelling a meaningful business outcome: prioritisation and risk.
* It makes Genie credible because non-technical leaders often need direct answers.
* It makes the final application feel like a mission tool rather than a demo widget.

## Capabilities to demonstrate

| Capability | Why it matters in the story | Audience takeaway |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ingestion | Legacy and landing-zone data arrive from different agencies and formats | Databricks unifies without demanding a big-bang rewrite |
| Transformation | Bronze, silver, and gold progression turns chaos into operational truth | Engineering discipline is built in |
| Data quality | Invalid records and missing fields create response risk | Trust is engineered, not assumed |
| Modelling | Incident severity or escalation risk can be predicted | AI is tied to outcomes, not novelty |
| Ontology | Shared definitions align agencies and executives | Semantic consistency matters as much as pipelines |
| Genie | Leaders and analysts can interrogate data in natural language | Access broadens beyond SQL users |
| UC gateway patterns | Existing systems can be queried and governed without crude replication | Governance and interoperability are platform strengths |
| Application | Frontline users consume decisions in context | Databricks reaches operational workflows |

## What to avoid

* long architecture lectures before the audience cares
* feature hopping with no story continuity
* excessive notebook code during the session
* synthetic examples that feel too retail or consumer-focused
* AI claims without governance, lineage, and human accountability

## Success criteria

By the end of the session, the audience should believe:
* Databricks can modernise public sector data estates incrementally
* governance and AI can coexist
* one platform can support both builders and consumers
* the path from raw data to decision support is coherent and realistic
